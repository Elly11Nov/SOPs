## 1. Purpose

This SOP defines the process for reviewing, approving and publishing controlled documents.

The process is designed to ensure that documents are reviewed by the appropriate stakeholders, required changes are addressed, approvals are recorded and only the approved version is published as the current document.

## 2. Scope

### 2.1 In Scope

This procedure applies to:

- New controlled documents.
- Major revisions to existing controlled documents.
- Documents requiring technical, business or compliance review.
- Documents that require formal approval before publication.

### 2.2 Out of Scope

The following are outside the scope of this SOP:

- Informal working notes and drafts that are not intended for controlled publication.
- Personal copies of documents.
- Minor editorial changes that do not require formal review under the applicable document-management process.

## 3. Roles and Responsibilities

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| **Document Owner** | Initiates the review, provides the source information, addresses review comments and ensures the document is ready for approval. |
| **Author** | Creates or updates the document and incorporates agreed changes. |
| **Technical Reviewer** | Verifies technical accuracy, terminology, examples and implementation details. |
| **Business Reviewer** | Confirms that the document reflects the intended business process, requirements or user needs. |
| **Approver** | Confirms that the document is complete and suitable for formal approval and publication. |
| **Document Controller** | Maintains document metadata, version information and publication status where this role exists. |

## 4. Procedure

### 4.1 Initiate the Review

1. Identify the document requiring review.
2. Confirm the document owner.
3. Determine the reason for the review, such as a new document, scheduled review, product change, process change or identified documentation issue.
4. Confirm the intended audience and purpose.
5. Identify the reviewers and approver required for the document.
6. Create or update the document using the approved template and naming convention.

### 4.2 Prepare the Document for Review

Before sending the document for review:

1. Confirm that the document has a clear purpose and scope.
2. Check that required sections are present.
3. Verify that examples, terminology and references are consistent.
4. Check that links and cross-references are valid.
5. Check that version and document metadata are complete.
6. Perform an editorial and formatting review.
7. Identify any known open issues or assumptions.

The document should be sufficiently complete for reviewers to assess its content rather than reviewing an unfinished working draft.

### 4.3 Conduct the Review

Send the document to the identified reviewers with a clear review deadline and instructions.

Reviewers should assess the document from the perspective of their assigned responsibility.

Typical review areas include:

| Review area | Example checks |
|---|---|
| Technical accuracy | Are technical statements, parameters, procedures and examples correct? |
| Business accuracy | Does the document reflect the intended process or business requirement? |
| Completeness | Is required information missing? |
| Consistency | Are terminology, naming and formatting consistent? |
| Usability | Can the intended audience understand and follow the information? |
| References | Are referenced documents, links and dependencies appropriate and current? |

### 4.4 Record Review Comments

Review comments should be recorded in a way that allows each issue to be tracked to resolution.

Where appropriate, comments should identify:

- The document section or location.
- The issue or requested change.
- The reason for the change.
- The reviewer.
- The status of the comment.

Comments should be specific enough for the document owner or author to understand the required action.

### 4.5 Resolve Review Comments

The document owner and author review the comments and determine the required action.

Each comment should be classified as one of the following:

- **Accepted** — the requested change will be incorporated.
- **Rejected** — the change will not be incorporated and the reason is recorded.
- **Deferred** — the issue is valid but will be addressed in a later revision.
- **Clarification required** — additional information is needed before the issue can be resolved.

Accepted changes must be incorporated into the document.

Where a comment is rejected or deferred, the decision should be documented when traceability is required.

### 4.6 Perform a Review of Changes

After review comments have been addressed:

1. Confirm that all required changes have been incorporated.
2. Confirm that rejected or deferred comments have been appropriately recorded.
3. Review affected sections for consistency.
4. Check cross-references and links again.
5. Check examples and supporting information.
6. Confirm that the document remains aligned with its purpose and scope.

A second technical or business review should be performed when the changes are significant or when required by the applicable document-management process.

### 4.7 Approve the Document

Once review comments have been resolved:

1. Submit the document to the designated approver.
2. The approver reviews the final version.
3. The approver confirms that the document is suitable for publication.
4. Record the approval using the applicable approval mechanism.
5. Record the approved version and effective date.

Approval should be performed only on the final version intended for publication.

### 4.8 Publish the Approved Version

After approval:

1. Publish the approved document to the designated documentation repository or publishing system.
2. Confirm that the published version matches the approved version.
3. Ensure that the previous version is archived or marked as superseded where required.
4. Verify that links and references to the document resolve correctly.
5. Communicate the availability of the updated document to affected stakeholders when required.

Only the approved version should be presented as the current controlled document.

### 4.9 Close the Review

The document owner closes the review when:

- Required reviews have been completed.
- Review comments have been resolved or appropriately documented.
- Approval has been recorded.
- The approved version has been published.
- Required previous versions or review records have been retained.

## 5. Review Quality Checks

Before closing the review, confirm:

- [ ] Purpose and scope are clear.
- [ ] Intended audience is identified.
- [ ] Required reviewers have completed their review.
- [ ] Technical content has been verified.
- [ ] Business/process content has been verified.
- [ ] Review comments have been resolved.
- [ ] Links and cross-references work.
- [ ] Examples are accurate and consistent.
- [ ] Version information is correct.
- [ ] Approval has been recorded.
- [ ] The published version matches the approved version.
- [ ] Superseded versions have been handled according to the applicable process.

## 6. Exceptions and Escalation

If a reviewer identifies an issue that cannot be resolved within the review cycle, the document owner should determine whether the document can proceed to approval.

Issues that affect technical accuracy, regulatory requirements, business-critical processes or user safety should be resolved before publication unless an authorised exception process explicitly permits otherwise.

## 7. Records and Document Control

Review and approval records should be retained according to the applicable document-management and records-retention requirements.

The controlled document should include sufficient metadata to identify its current version and approval status.

Changes made after approval should be handled through the applicable revision and review process rather than by modifying the approved version without traceability.

## 8. Continuous Improvement

Feedback from reviewers, users and document owners should be used to improve the review process.

Recurring review issues should be analysed to identify opportunities to improve:

- Documentation templates.
- Authoring guidance.
- Review checklists.
- Terminology standards.
- Information architecture.
- Review workflows.
- Training and onboarding materials.
