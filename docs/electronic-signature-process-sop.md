## 1. Purpose

This SOP describes how electronic signatures are applied to controlled records and how documents are migrated between handwritten and electronic signature formats.

The procedure is intended for personnel who perform or manage electronic signatures on controlled records. Electronic signatures may be used in regulated environments where the applicable system, process and controls meet the relevant requirements.

Where applicable, electronic signatures may be subject to requirements such as FDA 21 CFR Part 11 and organisational quality procedures.

## 2. Scope

### 2.1 In Scope

This document applies to:

- Controlled records that are approved using electronic signatures.
- Personnel who perform, verify or manage electronic signatures on those records.
- Processes in which a document changes between handwritten and electronic signature formats.

### 2.2 Out of Scope

Document types and signature processes that are not covered by the applicable electronic-signature policy or procedure.

## 4. Procedure

### 4.1 General Instructions

Before performing an electronic signature, the signer must complete any required acknowledgement or training defined by the applicable process.

### 4.2 Documents with Hybrid Signatures

A document may contain both handwritten and electronic signatures only when an approved process is used. Where required, a hybrid document must be migrated from one format to the other using the defined verification controls.

### 4.3 Migration to Electronic Format After a Handwritten Signature

1. Prepare the document.
2. Obtain the required handwritten signature(s).
3. Scan the document, including the pages containing handwritten signatures.
4. Have the scanned document verified against the original signed paper document for consistency.
5. Record the required verification statement or equivalent approved confirmation.
6. Complete the required electronic signature or approval.
7. Store the resulting electronic record in the defined controlled location.
8. Retain the paper document for the period defined by the applicable procedure, then dispose of it according to the approved retention process.

The verification should confirm that the electronic document is an accurate, complete and legible representation of the original paper document.

### 4.4 Migration to Electronic Format When a Handwritten Signature Is the Final Signature

Where a hybrid document has a handwritten signature as its final signature, local verification and migration to electronic format should be performed where the applicable process permits.

1. Prepare the document.
2. Complete the required electronic signatures.
3. Print the document for the required handwritten signature(s).
4. Verify the validity of the electronic signatures represented on the printed document.
5. Complete the handwritten approval.
6. Scan the complete document, including the electronic and handwritten signature pages.
7. Verify the scanned document against the signed paper document for consistency.
8. Store the resulting electronic record in the defined controlled location.

The applicable retention procedure determines how long the paper version must be retained after migration.

### 4.5 Migration to Paper Format When a Handwritten Signature Is the Final Signature

Paper-based hybrid records should normally be avoided where an approved electronic record can be maintained. If paper must become the official record, the change in signature method must be planned in advance.

1. Prepare the document.
2. Complete the required electronic signatures.
3. Print the document for the required handwritten signature(s).
4. Verify the validity of the electronic signatures represented on the printed document.
5. Complete the handwritten approval.
6. Retain the paper document as the official record when required by the applicable procedure.
7. Dispose of the electronic version according to the approved record-management and retention requirements.

### 4.6 Prohibited Process

The following process must not be used: a document is handwritten, scanned, sent electronically without verification against the original signed document, and then electronically signed without the required verification.

## 5. Controls and Monitoring

Controls are embedded within the procedure. Key controls include verification against the original signed document, completion of required approvals, controlled storage of the official record, and application of the applicable retention requirements.
